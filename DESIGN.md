## 1 · What it is and how it should feel
Parakeet: Budget Meal Planner — pick your store and your budget, get five dinners that share ingredients and one grocery list with the total added up before you shop. The audience: US home cooks on a budget, found on TikTok, reading on a phone, often at night, often tired.

The feeling in plain words: **calm, exact, on your side.** It should feel like a good receipt and a well-kept kitchen notebook, not like a startup. Numbers are the hero. Nothing shouts. One bird, one sky-blue, lots of air.

Not: Duolingo (no cartoon face, no streaks, no confetti). Not the 2026 AI page (no gradients, no glows, no purple, no cards inside cards, no icon on every line). Not a grocery flyer (no yellow bursts, no "SAVE!").

## 2 · Color — four tokens, nothing else
| token | value | role |
|---|---|---|
| sky | #4FA8E8 | the one accent: the icon's ground, the primary button, the selected state, the week's header field, the dinner card's day label |
| ink | #0E1B26 | all text (except Umyt's three exceptions below), the toast, the dark Pro icons |
| cream | #FBF6EC | the page and every setup screen's background |
| white | #FFFFFF | cards, rows, inputs |

Derived, never new colors: ink at 70% for secondary text (≈6:1 on cream), ink at 62% for hints and captions (≈4.5:1 on cream — the floor; never lighter for text), ink at 10% for hairlines, ink at 22% for strokes; sky-soft #E3F1FC for a selected row's fill. Text on sky is ink (≈6.7:1; white on sky is ≈2.6:1 and fails), and sky is not used for text on white or cream (≈2.6:1) — with three exceptions, Umyt's call (Oct 23), made knowing the numbers: the week's header is sky with white type, and the dinner card's day label is sky text on white (both in §5); and on the website, since its new look (Umyt, Nov 25), the footer's whole bird and giant wordmark are white on its sky, a logo, which contrast rules exempt (§4), in place of the reserve page's H1 and answer line in white, which the new look sets in ink. All three stay exactly as drawn: the header carries only the total, its "$", "est." and the one line "of your $N budget"; the day label is one word; on the website's sky, only the footer's bird and wordmark are white. No other text goes on sky or in sky; the button label stays ink. Dark mode later, from the same four.

Banned: any second hue (no greens, oranges, reds for meaning — "meatless", "over budget" are words, not colors), gradients (except the band in §5), glows, shadows under cards, tinted near-blacks.

## 3 · Type — the system font, hierarchy by weight and size
SF Pro in the app (`-apple-system`), the system stack on the page. Umyt's exception (Nov 4, kept Nov 6): the brand moments use the wordmark's letters, Outfit Bold, outlined so the app loads no font: the wordmark, the home's headline 'What's for dinner this week?' (on a past week, 'What was for dinner that week?'; Umyt, Nov 16), the bold line that takes its place, and every word beside the bird (Umyt, Nov 12): the welcome's word, the word after I cooked it (Umyt, Nov 11), and 'Congrats!' on the paywall, its only word (Umyt, Nov 12). No web font, no display face (Inter, Space Grotesk, Manrope, Bricolage, Poppins are the AI tell and stay out). Numbers always `tabular-nums`.

Scale (iPhone): display number 64–88 / 700 / tracking −0.04em · H1 28–34 / 700 / −0.02em / 1.1 · H2 20 / 700 · body 17 / 400 · row title 16–17 / 600 · secondary 15 / 400 at ink-70 · caption 13 / 400 at ink-62 · button 17 / 600. Only these sizes. Jumps are big (3×), not timid (1.5×).

Headlines are sentences with a period or a question mark. No all-caps labels anywhere. No "01 / 02 / 03" numbering. No "→" on buttons.

## 6 · Depth and motion
Flat. Depth comes from the cream/white/sky relationship and hairlines, never from shadows. Motion answers an action: screens slide in 220 ms; the total counts up once (900 ms) when the week is built; the meter fills once; the bird bobs only on the building screen. The one motion that doesn't answer an action, Umyt's: the website's answer line under the H1 crossfades through three answers once at load (about 2.5 s each, 400 ms fades) and then stays on the last — one pass, so no pause control is needed. The website's new look adds (Umyt, Nov 25): the phone's screen changes with the answer line, the week, the list, then the total, in 400 ms crossfades under the bezel; at load the first screen's tiles rise 16 px into place once, 600 ms on cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1), 100 ms apart, the words fading in as they rise, so the H1 moves once there and then never, and a tile with a phone only rises, so the bezel never fades; below the first screen each tile and the footer rise 14 px once, 500 ms on the same curve, the first time they scroll into view, and so do the headline 'Planning dinners for the whole week?' and its sentence, fading in as they rise, all visible at rest so nothing stays hidden if the script never runs; in the story, the line in focus turns ink in 200 ms and the phone's screen crossfades to its screen in 400 ms, ease-in-out, following the scroll with nothing tied to each pixel of it; the reserve sheet rises in 320 ms on the same curve while the page dims in 200 ms, a computer's dialog rises 12 px as it fades in, 240 ms, a plan's ring and fill change in 150 ms and its tick pops, 80% to 112% and back in 260 ms, as the app's ticks do, the next step crossfades in 200 ms with a 4 px rise, the done line rises 4 px in 200 ms, and closing slides the sheet down in 320 ms, while a computer's dialog closes as it opened, dropping 12 px as it fades out in 240 ms, the dim fading out in 200 ms on both; and on an Android phone the band in §5 crosses its button 3 times, 1.2 s each, 0.6 s apart, from 1 s after load, then rests, since on a web page motion that runs past 5 s needs a pause control (WCAG 2.2.2). With Reduce Motion nothing on the website moves: the last answer and the total show, the story's lines and screens change at once, and the sheet and its steps appear at once. Nothing else moves. Reduced motion turns all of it off (the last answer shows, still).

## 7 · The bird (asset brief)
The mark: the clean white parakeet silhouette, its eye one solid oval and its beak shown by its outline alone, with no lines inside (parakeet-mark.svg, currentColor; Umyt, Nov 12). It appears exactly here and nowhere else: the app icon (the head close up, white on sky, as much space above the head as after the beak, 16% of the icon each; Umyt, Nov 12), the welcome screen (sky, 44 px, beside the wordmark), the building screen (sky, 96 px, bobbing), the week's header (white, 48 px, corner), empty states, the trial reminder, the video end card, the website (Umyt, Nov 25): the app's icon beside the wordmark at the top of each page, and the whole white bird beside the giant wordmark in its footer (§4). Never on a money row, never as a mascot with limbs, never repeated down a screen (the website, which shows the bird at its top and in its footer; Umyt, Nov 25). Pro icons: the same icon in five colorways (sky bird on cream, ink bird on cream, white on ink, gold on ink, ink on lemon).

Device frames: a realistic iPhone (outer frame, Dynamic Island, side buttons) only as Apple's own product bezel from developer.apple.com/design/resources, used as is — Apple's marketing guidelines forbid cropping, tilting, obstructing, adding shadows or reflections to it, and forbid drawing or simulating an Apple product; the screen shows the app as it runs; at least 200 px tall on screen; the latest iPhone. Anything that has to be cropped or faded uses a generic frame with no Apple details.

Any other asset needs a one-line brief before it exists: what it is for, where it sits, its size, what it should make the person notice. If it only decorates, it is removed. Feature glyphs (welcome screen) are simple 2 px strokes in ink on a sky disc, three at most, drawn by Umyt or as plain SVG — never an icon pack.

Photos (the page's floating staples, later the recipe photos): Umyt's own phone photos first. For Gate 0 only, Pixabay photos are allowed (its Content License: commercial use, no credit required, editing allowed) on four conditions: a real photo, never one marked "AI-generated"; no visible trademark, logo or brand (Pixabay's own license bars commercial use of those); it matches its tag; its Pixabay page link is logged in CREDITS.md in the repo (Pixabay does not warrant its uploaders' rights, so the link is the record). They are replaced by Umyt's own photos at launch. Never other stock (no watermarked comps, no Freepik, iStock, Dreamstime or Shutterstock files, no cutouts saved from other sites), never AI-made food — the page promises real recipes from real people, and the audience hates AI in food. Each photo shows exactly the item its tag names: a yellow onion, a 2 lb bag of white rice, a can of black beans, a block of cheddar, flour tortillas, limes, a bell pepper, potatoes. Labels turned away from the camera. Shot on a white sheet or paper in window light from one side, no flash, one item per photo filling about half the frame, straight down for flat items and 45° for round ones; sent with backgrounds as they are; cut out by Claude so every edge and scale matches. A price tag carries the regular price Umyt typed from the Walmart app, with the date, marked "est." — never another app's number, and never a sale price (when the app shows "Now $X, was $Y", the price is $Y). Dish photos for the cards are 16:10 (720 px for the mock, 480 px for the page). Editing stays honest: crop to the frame; when a photo is tighter than 16:10, the plain background (a table, a cloth) may be extended at the sides from the photo's own background — copied patches, never AI fill, never the food or the plate; a photo on a white background is padded with white; tone corrections stay light (the food keeps its real color); every edit is noted beside its link in CREDITS.md.

## 8 · Do / Don't
Do: real copy on every screen (the words in the mock are the words) · one accent · big quiet numbers · the stove-and-notebook calm · test every screen at SE size and on a dark wallpaper · look at the icon in the App Store grid · put the real result screen where a stranger looks first · write copy as plain, full sentences, the way you'd say them: what is true today, or "will" / "plan to" for what isn't yet, and a line that depends on the plan someone picked says so (Oct 23).
Don't: gradients (except the band in §5), glows, blur, shadows, purple, a second hue, cards in cards, icons on every row, badges for no reason, all-caps labels, numbered eyebrows, a display font, a logo repeated through the app, text in the icon, a social-proof number we don't have, a store's logo, "AI" anywhere in the copy except the one promise "None will be made by AI." (never AI as a feature or a selling point), stock photos or AI-made food, a photo that doesn't match its tag, small text below 4.5:1 (Umyt's three exceptions in §2 aside), a drawn iPhone, an Apple bezel that is cropped, faded or overlapped, a headline that moves (except the website's H1, which rises once with its tile at load, and its headline below the first screen, which rises once as it comes into view, Umyt's, Nov 25), copy in fragments, colon reveals or middle-dot strings ("Regular shelf prices, whole packs.", "Swapped · list unchanged").

## 9 · How a screen gets made (the loop)
1. The plan first: the screen's job, its one question, the real words, which components it uses — written before any code.
2. Build with the tokens only (CSS variables; no new values).
3. Screenshot at 393×852 and 375×667, light; the result screen also on a dark wallpaper.
4. Critique against §2–§8, line by line, not against taste: every rule broken is a fix, and nothing else is a fix.
5. Fix, screenshot again. Three rounds at most; then it ships or the plan changes.
6. Umyt looks last and says what is wrong in one sentence per screen.
