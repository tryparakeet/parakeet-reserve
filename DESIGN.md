## 1 · What it is and how it should feel
Parakeet: Budget Meal Planner — pick your store and your budget, get five dinners that share ingredients and one grocery list with the total added up before you shop. The audience: US home cooks on a budget, found on TikTok, reading on a phone, often at night, often tired.

The feeling in plain words: **calm, exact, on your side.** It should feel like a good receipt and a well-kept kitchen notebook, not like a startup. Numbers are the hero. Nothing shouts. One bird, one sky-blue, lots of air.

Not: Duolingo (no cartoon face, no streaks, no confetti). Not the 2026 AI page (no gradients, no glows, no purple, no cards inside cards, no icon on every line). Not a grocery flyer (no yellow bursts, no "SAVE!").

## 2 · Color — four tokens, nothing else
| token | value | role |
|---|---|---|
| sky | #4FA8E8 | the one accent: the icon's ground, the primary button, the selected state, the week's header field, the dinner card's day label |
| ink | #0E1B26 | all text (except Umyt's three exceptions below), the toast, the dark Pro icons |
| cream | #FBF6EC | the page and every setup screen's background |
| white | #FFFFFF | cards, rows, inputs |

Derived, never new colors: ink at 70% for secondary text (≈6:1 on cream), ink at 62% for hints and captions (≈4.5:1 on cream — the floor; never lighter for text), ink at 10% for hairlines, ink at 22% for strokes; sky-soft #E3F1FC for a selected row's fill. Text on sky is ink (≈6.7:1; white on sky is ≈2.6:1 and fails), and sky is not used for text on white or cream (≈2.6:1) — with three exceptions, Umyt's call (Oct 23), made knowing the numbers: the week's header is sky with white type, and the dinner card's day label is sky text on white (both in §5); and the reserve page's H1 with its answer line is white on the sky field, centered — the H1 34/700 in white, the answer line 17/400 at white 72% (§4). All three stay exactly as drawn: the header carries only the total, its "$", "est." and the one line "of your $N budget"; the day label is one word; on the page's sky, only the wordmark, the H1 and the answer line. No other text goes on sky or in sky; the button label stays ink. Dark mode later, from the same four.

Banned: any second hue (no greens, oranges, reds for meaning — "meatless", "over budget" are words, not colors), gradients, glows, shadows under cards, tinted near-blacks.

## 3 · Type — the system font, hierarchy by weight and size
SF Pro in the app (`-apple-system`), the system stack on the page. No web font, no display face (Inter, Space Grotesk, Manrope, Bricolage, Poppins are the AI tell and stay out). Numbers always `tabular-nums`.

Scale (iPhone): display number 64–88 / 700 / tracking −0.04em · H1 28–34 / 700 / −0.02em / 1.1 · H2 20 / 700 · body 17 / 400 · row title 16–17 / 600 · secondary 15 / 400 at ink-70 · caption 13 / 400 at ink-62 · button 17 / 600. Only these sizes. Jumps are big (3×), not timid (1.5×).

Headlines are sentences with a period or a question mark. No all-caps labels anywhere. No "01 / 02 / 03" numbering. No "→" on buttons.

## 4 · Layout
Phone first, one column, 20 px side gutter, 8-pt grid (every padding and gap is a multiple of 4; most are 8, 12, 16, 20). Everything left-aligned except the big number on the budget screen. The primary button sits at the bottom, safe-area aware, always reachable with a thumb. One screen = one question. Lists are grouped white cards with hairline separators (the iOS grouped-list shape), not individual floating cards.

The first screen of the reserve page is the whole page: wordmark, the H1 "What's for dinner this week?", the answer line, the real result screen in a phone frame, the button with one line under it (Umyt's own words, as on the artboard). The other proof lines live below the fold. No scroll needed at SE size (667 pt); the phone frame shrinks first, the copy never. The reserve page's first screen is hero B (picked Oct 22): the top is a sky field holding the wordmark, the H1 and the answer line; the phone sits between the sky and the cream above a white bottom sheet that holds the button. Exceptions there, Umyt's: the wordmark is white on the sky (a logotype — contrast rules exempt logos); the phone is Apple's bezel (uploaded Oct 22), whole and straight with nothing over it and the white sheet below it (a sheet fading onto the phone was allowed only with a generic frame, §7); the phone's screen shows the week's header in sky with white type, exactly as the app does; the H1 and the answer line are white on the sky and centered (Umyt's canvas edit of Oct 22, kept by his call on Oct 23 — the §2 exception). Centered exceptions: hero A's H1 (kept on the canvas, not shipped); the app's welcome screen (W2) — the bird, the H1, one line, the button; and Umyt's hand edits on the page (Oct 22): hero B's H1 and answer line, the line under the button, the email step's title and small print, and the done message; and on the sheet, by Umyt's "center them all" (Oct 23): the plan sheet's title and small print. Headlines use `text-wrap: balance` so no line ends on one word.

Below the first screen (Umyt's pick C1, Oct 23): the Reserve sheet (the button and Umyt's line) stays pinned to the bottom of the screen while the page scrolls under it, so the button is always one thumb away and nothing else sits inside the sheet. Everything below the first screen is on cream: three rows with a 76 px label column — Stores, Prices, Recipes; labels 15/600 at ink-62, values 17/25 in ink, hairlines between — then the footer under a hairline (15/22 at ink-70; links 15/600 ink, 44 px targets; "© 2026 Parakeet." 13 at ink-62). The stores are chips in our own type (white pill, ink-22 ring, 15/600 ink, 32 px tall): Walmart, Kroger, Target, Aldi, then "More soon" (a dashed ink-22 outline, ink-62, weight 500), because a plain "More" would claim stores we don't price yet. Never a store's logo: Walmart's own trademark terms require its prior written permission for its logos, and App Review guideline 5.2.1 rejects third-party trademarks used without permission.

## 5 · Components (the style-guide page shows every one of these in every state)
- **Primary button** — pill (999 px radius), 56 px tall, sky with ink text 17/600; pressed = 6% darker; disabled = ink-10 fill with ink at 48% text (disabled controls are the one place below 4.5:1). One per screen.
- **Secondary action** — text only, ink 16/600, pill hit area, no border ("None of these", "Nothing to skip").
- **Selection row** — white, 14 px radius, hairline border; selected = sky-soft fill + 1.5 px sky inset stroke + a sky tick with a white check. Single-choice lists and multi-choice lists look the same; the difference is behavior.
- **Chip** — pill, white, hairline; selected like the row. Only for short multi-choice lists (allergies, skip foods, eating style).
- **Stepper** — 64 px round buttons, the number 76/700 between them.
- **Switch** — iOS shape, sky when on.
- **Slider** — 8 px track, sky fill to the thumb, white 32 px thumb with a hairline.
- **Progress** — 4 px bar, sky, top of the screen; hidden on welcome, building and result.
- **The week's header** (H1, re-picked by Umyt Oct 23 — pick A, replacing the ink H2) — a sky field, bottom corners 22 px; the total as the display number in white, its "$" at white 78%, an "est." tag in white text with a 1 px white-60 outline; one line under it, "of your $N budget", at white 78% with the amount in white; a meter with a white fill on a white-34 track; the bird in white, 48 px, top-right. No eyebrow line: the store, the household and the nights are not repeated here (Umyt: extra words). White on sky is ≈2.6:1 — the §2 exception; nothing else is added to this field. This is the hero of the app, the page and the clip.
- **Dinner card** (D1 reworked by Umyt, Oct 22; day label moved Oct 23) — a white card, radius 22, hairline: a header row with the dish 16/600 on the left and its day on the right of the same line — the full word ("Monday"), sky 15/600, no pill or fill (the §2 exception); under the dish, "$2.60 a serving · 25 min" 13 at ink-62 (a serving price needs the recipe's amounts — what share of each pack that dinner uses — so it appears with the recipes, never as the week's total divided by five; until then the time alone); the dish photo below (16:10, radius 14, a 1 px ink-10 inner hairline so a photo on a white background still reads as a photo); an action row: heart (like — "more like this"; filled sky when on), speech bubble (feedback on the dinner), and "…" at the far right opening a sheet: Swap this dinner · Don't show this again · Cancel. Plain stroke icons, 1.8 px, 24 px in 44 px targets; no counts. Photos follow §7 (real, source shown; placeholders in the mock until then).
- **List row** (L2, picked Oct 22) — check first: a 22 px circle that fills sky with a white check when ticked, the item 15, the pack 13 at ink-62, the price 15/600 tabular at ink-70 on the right; a ticked line goes ink-62 and struck; "you have it" lines show ticked without a price. One component for planning and for the store.
- **Toast** — ink pill (radius 20 when it wraps to two lines), white 14/600, bottom, 1.6 s; always a full sentence ("Swapped. Same groceries, so your list didn't change.").

## 6 · Depth and motion
Flat. Depth comes from the cream/white/sky relationship and hairlines, never from shadows. Motion answers an action: screens slide in 220 ms; the total counts up once (900 ms) when the week is built; the meter fills once; the bird bobs only on the building screen. The one motion that doesn't answer an action, Umyt's: the page's answer line under the H1 crossfades through three answers once at load (about 2.5 s each, 400 ms fades) and then stays on the last — one pass, so no pause control is needed; the H1 never moves. One more, Umyt's (Oct 23): below the first screen, each row and the footer fade in once, the first time they scroll into view (500 ms, a 14 px rise; visible at rest, so nothing stays hidden if the script never runs). Nothing else moves. Reduced motion turns all of it off (the last answer shows, still).

## 7 · The bird (asset brief)
The mark: the clean white parakeet silhouette with the eye highlight (parakeet-mark.svg, currentColor). It appears exactly here and nowhere else: the app icon (white on sky), the welcome screen (sky, 44 px, beside the wordmark), the building screen (sky, 96 px, bobbing), the week's header (white, 48 px, corner), empty states, the trial reminder, the video end card, the page's wordmark. Never on a money row, never as a mascot with limbs, never repeated down a screen. Pro icons: the same mark in five colorways (sky bird on cream, ink bird on cream, white on ink, gold on ink, ink on lemon).

Device frames: a realistic iPhone (outer frame, Dynamic Island, side buttons) only as Apple's own product bezel from developer.apple.com/design/resources, used as is — Apple's marketing guidelines forbid cropping, tilting, obstructing, adding shadows or reflections to it, and forbid drawing or simulating an Apple product; the screen shows the app as it runs; at least 200 px tall on screen; the latest iPhone. Anything that has to be cropped or faded uses a generic frame with no Apple details.

Any other asset needs a one-line brief before it exists: what it is for, where it sits, its size, what it should make the person notice. If it only decorates, it is removed. Feature glyphs (welcome screen) are simple 2 px strokes in ink on a sky disc, three at most, drawn by Umyt or as plain SVG — never an icon pack.

Photos (the page's floating staples, later the recipe photos): Umyt's own phone photos first. For Gate 0 only, Pixabay photos are allowed (its Content License: commercial use, no credit required, editing allowed) on four conditions: a real photo, never one marked "AI-generated"; no visible trademark, logo or brand (Pixabay's own license bars commercial use of those); it matches its tag; its Pixabay page link is logged in CREDITS.md in the repo (Pixabay does not warrant its uploaders' rights, so the link is the record). They are replaced by Umyt's own photos at launch. Never other stock (no watermarked comps, no Freepik, iStock, Dreamstime or Shutterstock files, no cutouts saved from other sites), never AI-made food — the page promises real recipes from real people, and the audience hates AI in food. Each photo shows exactly the item its tag names: a yellow onion, a 2 lb bag of white rice, a can of black beans, a block of cheddar, flour tortillas, limes, a bell pepper, potatoes. Labels turned away from the camera. Shot on a white sheet or paper in window light from one side, no flash, one item per photo filling about half the frame, straight down for flat items and 45° for round ones; sent with backgrounds as they are; cut out by Claude so every edge and scale matches. A price tag carries the regular price Umyt typed from the Walmart app, with the date, marked "est." — never another app's number, and never a sale price (when the app shows "Now $X, was $Y", the price is $Y). Dish photos for the cards are 16:10 (720 px for the mock, 480 px for the page). Editing stays honest: crop to the frame; when a photo is tighter than 16:10, the plain background (a table, a cloth) may be extended at the sides from the photo's own background — copied patches, never AI fill, never the food or the plate; a photo on a white background is padded with white; tone corrections stay light (the food keeps its real color); every edit is noted beside its link in CREDITS.md.

## 8 · Do / Don't
Do: real copy on every screen (the words in the mock are the words) · one accent · big quiet numbers · the stove-and-notebook calm · test every screen at SE size and on a dark wallpaper · look at the icon in the App Store grid · put the real result screen where a stranger looks first · write copy as plain, full sentences, the way you'd say them: what is true today, or "will" / "plan to" for what isn't yet, and a line that depends on the plan someone picked says so (Oct 23).
Don't: gradients, glows, blur, shadows, purple, a second hue, cards in cards, icons on every row, badges for no reason, all-caps labels, numbered eyebrows, a display font, a logo repeated through the app, text in the icon, a social-proof number we don't have, a store's logo, "AI" anywhere in the copy except the one promise "None will be made by AI." (never AI as a feature or a selling point), stock photos or AI-made food, a photo that doesn't match its tag, small text below 4.5:1 (Umyt's three exceptions in §2 aside), a drawn iPhone, an Apple bezel that is cropped, faded or overlapped, a headline that moves, copy in fragments, colon reveals or middle-dot strings ("Regular shelf prices, whole packs.", "Swapped · list unchanged").

## 9 · How a screen gets made (the loop)
1. The plan first: the screen's job, its one question, the real words, which components it uses — written before any code.
2. Build with the tokens only (CSS variables; no new values).
3. Screenshot at 393×852 and 375×667, light; the result screen also on a dark wallpaper.
4. Critique against §2–§8, line by line, not against taste: every rule broken is a fix, and nothing else is a fix.
5. Fix, screenshot again. Three rounds at most; then it ships or the plan changes.
6. Umyt looks last and says what is wrong in one sentence per screen.
